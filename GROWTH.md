# Growth

The growth PM's log for Parlo. Claude wears this hat in every product discussion and keeps this file current: the north star, the principles, the experiment backlog ("shots at bat") and a dated decision log. Agents: read this before any product or UX discussion, append a dated entry after one, and move experiments through the statuses. Never rewrite history; add an entry.

## North star: the loop

Measure the loop, not the survey. Response count is a vanity metric once the agent is in the room.

```
report delivered → report opened → answered the next round → shared the report → started their own parlo
```

The three ratios that matter, in order:

1. reveal → answered the next round (retention)
2. reveal → shared back to the group (virality)
3. reveal → started their own parlo (the flywheel)

Funnel events today (frontend `trackEvent`, backend PostHog, project 653180): `group_report_viewed`, `participant_joined_group`, `consent_accepted {round, visibility}`, `participant_submitted`, `participant_share_survey_clicked`, `survey_creation_started`, `agent_digest_completed`, `agent_round_proposed`, `round_approved`.

Missing, to add with the episode work: `gate_shown`, `wait_shown`, `nudge_tapped`, `reveal_shown`, `report_shared`, `start_own_tapped {from}`. Instrument transitions, not screens.

## Principles

Applied from Nikita Bier's consumer-social playbook (full notes in Claude's memory, `reference_nikita_bier_playbook`).

- **One community first.** Pick a single WhatsApp-heavy group and be obnoxiously relevant to it. If it works in one it works in all; if it fails in three it fails everywhere.
- **The artefact that leaves the app beats the page inside it.** Parlo lives inside WhatsApp groups. The report image posted back to the group is the product's face; the web page is where you answer and get the reveal.
- **Gate and reveal are moments, not list items.** "Answer to unlock" is a curiosity cliff. The unlock is full-screen. Never flatten either into a feed.
- **Toilet test.** Each answer under 30 seconds. If it can't be done distracted, it won't become a habit.
- **Habit is exposure on other networks.** Nobody returns to a destination unprompted. Rounds, nudges and report posts are the re-entry points.
- **Shots at bat.** A reproducible test process beats any single idea. Every bet below ships with a hypothesis, one metric, a kill criterion and a review date.
- **Peer visibility drives completion.** In open groups, who hasn't answered is the retention engine. In private groups, the count is.

### 2026-10-25 · The outputs deck: content types, not channels

**Context.** Andrej looked at the outputs screen (Google Sheet, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, Google Drive, Agent-ready URL, Podcast, Somewhere else) and asked whether it should be organised by content type rather than channel. Discussion and sketches only, nothing changed.

**What the channels actually take.** Instagram: photo, carousel, Reel, Story, Live. LinkedIn: text post, image, multi-image, document carousel, video, poll, article, newsletter. TikTok: vertical video, photo mode (slideshow), text, Story. YouTube: long video, Short, podcast, community post. X and Threads: text, thread, images, video, poll. Podcast apps: audio episode. Across all of them there are about nine primitives (short text, long text, single image, carousel, short vertical video, long video, audio episode, poll, ephemeral story), and each primitive has a home on several channels: one carousel file goes to Instagram, LinkedIn and TikTok; one 9:16 clip goes to Reels, TikTok and Shorts.

**Why content type wins.** The current card is doing two jobs: picking a channel and shaping the ask ("Instagram: carousel, adds a photo ask"). The content type is what determines the ask and what the agent has to be able to make; the channel is only where the file ends up, and that is already a separate concept (the Exports card, MCP destinations). Two things fall out for voice-first: a carousel of quote cards and a post need no new ask, a voice answer gives the quotable line and the transcript; and short vertical video does not need a video ask either, since an audiogram (waveform and captions over a still) is native on Reels, TikTok, Shorts and LinkedIn. The photo and video asks become optional enrichments, not requirements.

**Leaning.** Six cards, same anatomy as the shape screen, in this order: **Google Sheet & Drive** (answers and raw files; one card, on by default, first so the screen stays a pass-through for the people who only want that), **Clips** (short audio & video clips, second: the one output nobody else makes from a WhatsApp group, and it sells the voice-note format), **Post** (their best lines, in your voice), **Thread** (one take per person, in order), **Carousel** (quote cards to swipe through), **Episode** (their voices, edited into one). Heading "What should it make?", subline "From everyone's answers. The report always comes back." Nothing else on the screen: no channel glyphs on the cards (sketched, too messy), no ask tags (the ask-shaping happens silently when a card is picked), no Article (long reads are not what a group of voice notes makes), no Somewhere else and no Agent-ready URL (destinations, they live in the Exports card after the build). Sketched through eight, seven and six-card versions; six is the pick.

**Forces.** `outputs` ids become `sheet | clips | post | thread | carousel | episode` in `frontend/src/lib/outputs.ts` and `backend/src/services/outputs.ts`, with the retired channel ids still accepted for old parlos the way the retired shape ids are; the "Also make" brief lines move with them (post and thread = one quotable line per person; carousel = one optional photo ask; clips = one optional video ask, else an audiogram; episode = voice-only told moments); the shape defaults (the three self-promotion shapes pre-pick Post and Carousel next to the Sheet); `create-flow.spec.ts`; S19's pick counts keep working under the new ids.

**Open.** Whether Thread earns its slot once S19 has counts (it is the one card that is a format of the same material as Post). Clips says "audio & video" so a creator knows it works from voice alone; the audiogram is the audio half. Which producer comes first: the Sheet is the only one with a destination today, and the audiogram is the cheapest of the publish formats to make in a Worker.

**Shipped, same day.** The six-card deck on `feature/content-type-outputs-deck`: the two vocabularies, `LEGACY_OUTPUTS` on the backend mapping the channel ids to their content type (so an old parlo's `/brief.md` now says Carousel where it said Instagram carousel), the three post shapes pre-picking Post and Carousel beside the Sheet, the heading and subline from the sketch, the brand glyphs for Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok and Drive removed, `create-flow.spec.ts` on three devices. No producer exists for any card; S19's pick counts continue under the new ids (the old events carry the old ids, so compare by content type when reading them).

### 2026-10-25 · The third row, built: deadlines, the check-in cadence, follow-ups

**Context.** Andrej looked at the check-in's sentence screen and asked where the third row went. It had only ever been sketched (the entry below); nothing was built because the entry's own rule was "don't ship the chip without at least the close".

**Shipped.** The row and the close together, on `feature/round-deadlines`. On the sentence screen the third row is the shape's own question, only where the chip changes what the agent does: "When do you need it?" (Today / This week / Keep it open, This week on) for post, testimonials, milestone, feedback and team; "How often?" (Every day / Every week) for the check-in; "Follow up?" The deadline shows on the welcome screen ("Closes in 3 days. **Auto-send, decided the same day.** Andrej: the check-in sends its next round without approval. ### 2026-10-19 · How conditionals should work

**Context.** Andrej asked how branching logic would work in this flow. Discussion only.

**Discussed.** Parlo already branches between rounds: the agent's round-2 probes aimed at members by what they said, approved by the owner. What is missing is branching inside a round. ### 2026-10-19 · The loop: the agent asks in taps, you fidget, it asks again

**Context.** Andrej, thinking aloud over the angles row: the agent starts with little context but it can ask for it, the way "Sharpen it" does today; then it should drop into something fidgetable; and when the creator signals "this doesn't make sense" it goes back into context capture. "It reminds me of I spy with my little eye: you have to ask a very targeted, limited set of questions."

**Discussed.** The I spy framing fixes the one thing wrong with capture today: "Sharpen it" asks open questions ("what's the budget range?") that want a sentence back, which is the performance the 2026-10-19 entry above rules out. In I spy, Twenty Questions or Akinator the asker carries the burden: closed questions, each one chosen to cut the space in half, each answer a word. So **capture is taps too.** The agent asks "Is this your team or your customers?", "Do they already know the options, or are you collecting them?", "Does this end in a decision, or is it to hear people out?", "Anything that would make an answer useless to you?" with two to four chips, an Other and a Skip. The difference between the two modes is not the input, it is whose turn it is: in capture the agent leads and the creator picks; in fidget the creator pokes at the artefact.

Three consequences:
1. *One screen, two turns.* Capture is a card above the deck, not a separate mode: the agent's question on top, the angle deck underneath, and the deck reflows as each chip is tapped (the IKEA effect of watching your taps shape the thing). When the agent has nothing to ask, the card is gone and only the deck remains. "Not quite right" on the deck brings the card back with one question about what was just touched: turn off budget and dates and it asks "Looks like the where is settled. Is this about picking between the spots, or about timing?"
2. *The question types are finite and topic-agnostic.* Who they are; what is already known (options given vs. collected); whether it ends in a decision; constraints; how sensitive; how deep. The topic only fills in the options. That is why the offsite and the G2 flow are the same machine: G2 is Get feedback × customers × "our product against the alternatives", and the capture asks "current customers or churned?", "scores or stories?", "public like G2 or internal?", and the deck becomes what they use it for, what it replaced, the best and worst thing, would they recommend, is the price fair. The brief checklist's five items (audience, goal, purpose, tone, length) are already this list, and `brief/evaluate` already judges which are missing, so the agent has a cheap signal for *which* questions to ask and *whether* to ask at all.
3. *A budget.* I spy is a limited set. Two to four questions per capture entry, announced ("Three quick ones and I'll have it"), Skip always, and the agent stops early when the deck has stopped moving. Ask-first or deck-first is decided by the agent's own confidence: a specific blank goes straight to the deck with an offer to ask; a thin or ambiguous one gets the card first with the deck visible under it. Send is live in both.

**Why it matters for growth.** The capture card is where the agent first looks smart rather than like a form builder, and that is the moment people talk about. It is also one interaction language for the whole product: the round-2 proposal on the home list, the member's "what should it have asked?", and the participant interview are all the agent asking, you tapping, the thing changing. The guardrail is the Other rate: generated options that miss are worse than a blank.

S28 and S29 added; "Sharpen it" is what S28 replaces.

**Open.** Whether two chips or four is the right default per question (two is faster, four is more informative). Whether implicit triggers annoy (start with the explicit button only). What the agent says when the Other rate on a parlo is high (fall back to one open question, by voice, as the last resort). Whether the capture card should carry the agent's confidence at all, or just the count.

### 2026-10-15 · Beyond the review screen: what is it for?

**Context.** Andrej, growth and UX hats on, nothing to build: the review screen (four cards, Shorter / More playful / Sharpen it, "Confirm & go live") may be the drop-off point. People agree on a goal easily; the shape and blank screens are about the goal. Agreeing on the exact set of questions is hard, and the creator is doing it alone, for a group that isn't in the room, before anyone has answered. He likes that the flow produces an IKEA effect and wants to keep that.

**Discussed.** What the screen is for today: trust (what will my friends be asked), correction, and the IKEA effect. Only the third needs a list of questions, and a list is the worst way to get the first two: reading a question is abstract, you can't tell if it's right until someone has to answer it, and "live" makes the tap heavy. The question set is the model's artefact, the goal is the human's; the screen asks the human to sign off on the model's artefact with no information to judge it by. Six directions, from least to most brave:
1. *Send an invite, not approve a questionnaire* (S23). The link is ready at build; the screen shows what the group will see (the WhatsApp preview card, the welcome, the report shape) with the questions collapsed under it; "Send to the group"; editable until the first answer lands.
2. *One question in round 1* (S21). The length chip's "One question" as the default; review is one card; round 2 is proposed from real answers, which is where questions get good. Shortest path to a first reveal, more rounds, more re-entry points.
3. *The group co-authors* (S22). After the goal, the group fixes the questions: a fixed last ask "What should it have asked?" feeds the round-2 proposal. The creator no longer has to be right alone. The cheap version of a "suggest a question" surface for members.
4. *Try it first* (S20). The review *is* answering your own parlo by voice: welcome, the asks, "Change this one" after each, thank-you = share. Reading becomes doing, a bad question is obvious the moment you have to answer it, the creator's answer is answer #1 (no empty room), and they see a report-of-one before anyone else. The IKEA effect comes from effort on the thing itself, not from fidgeting with a list. Costs about two minutes of voice; "Skip, just send it" for the impatient.
